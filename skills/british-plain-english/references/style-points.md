# Style points

House-style rules adapted from the GOV.UK A to Z style guide. Use them in documents. In a chat reply, use the dates, times, numbers and abbreviations rules.

## Dates and times

- dates: 2 June 2026, with no ordinal (not 2nd June) and no comma before the year
- days: Monday 2 June 2026
- ranges: "2 June to 6 June 2026", "Monday to Friday", "tax year 2025 to 2026" – use "to", not a dash
- deadlines: "on or before 30 June 2026"
- times: 9am, 5:30pm, with no space and no full stops
- noon and midnight: "midday", "midnight", not 12pm, 12am or 00:00
- time zones: give the zone when readers are in more than one, for example "14:02 UTC" in an incident report

## Numbers

- write "one" in prose, but use numerals for every other number, including 2 to 9
- use numerals for steps, versions, dates and measurements: "step 1", "version 3"
- use commas in numbers over 999: 10,000
- percentages: 12%
- ranges: "500 to 900"
- ordinals: first to ninth in words, 10th and above in numerals
- money: £75, £1.50, £2 million, 50p – no "£2.00" unless amounts sit together in a table
- temperature: 21°C

## Abbreviations

- write the full form the first time, with the abbreviation in brackets: "Transport Layer Security (TLS)"
- no full stops in abbreviations: BBC, UK, Dr, Mr, am, pm
- do not use eg, ie or etc – write "for example", "such as", "like", "including" or "that is", or name every item
- do not end a list that starts with "for example" or "such as" with "etc"

## Punctuation

- single quotes for a term the reader needs but does not know yet, at its first mention only: the 'idempotency key'
- single quotes for interface labels and titles: the 'Save' button, the 'Getting started' guide
- double quotes only for a direct quotation of what someone said or wrote
- round brackets (like this), and square brackets only for placeholders: [your project name]
- do not write "file(s)" – use the plural, "files"
- "and", not "&", unless it is part of a name
- no semicolons
- no em dashes – a spaced en dash ( – ) is fine for a short aside in a list or table, but in prose write 2 sentences
- hyphens: co-operate, co-ordinate, re-evaluate, but email, online, reuse, reopen, login (noun) and log in (verb)

## Lists

A bullet list needs:

- a lead-in line that ends in a colon
- at least 2 bullets
- bullets that each read on from the lead-in line
- a lower-case letter at the start of each bullet
- one sentence or phrase per bullet
- no "and" or "or" after a bullet, and no semicolon at the end of a bullet
- no full stop after the last bullet

Numbered steps are different. Use them for a process the reader follows in order. Each step is a full sentence with a capital letter and a full stop. Steps do not need a lead-in line.

## Headings and titles

- sentence case for every heading and title: "Set up the database", not "Set Up The Database"
- describe the content and front-load the important word: "Fix connection timeouts", not "Troubleshooting"
- start with a verb where you can: "Install sqlpipe"
- never write a heading as a question
- do not use a technical term in a heading before you explain it
- the page still makes sense with the headings removed

## Links

- the link text says where it goes: "read the installation guide", not "click here"
- front-load the link text and keep it short
- write email addresses in full, in lower case

## People and tone

- address the reader as "you"
- use "they" for a person whose gender you do not know
- no "please", no exclamation marks, no block capitals for emphasis
- brisk, but not terse: keep articles and full grammar
